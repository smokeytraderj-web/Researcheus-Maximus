Start with performance, then check actual co-movement. Every dot is a stock. Select a colored group to highlight its members across all three graphs.

01 / Return vs. score

Higher = stronger technical score. Further right = a larger gain over the past year. Color marks correlation groups; larger points have higher scores. Similar scores or yearly gains alone do not mean stocks are correlated.

Hover or select a point for stock details.

How to read this graph

Higher means a stronger current setup; further right means a larger past gain. Upper-right stocks have both. Upper-left stocks have stronger scores despite weak past returns. The shaded band marks scores of 8–10. A current score contains trend and momentum inputs, so its relationship to past returns is partly built into the model; this graph does not validate future returns.

The 100/200-day view retains your original comparison. The most recent 100 days are contained in the 200-day window, so the axes share data. The preceding-100-day option separates the windows: upper-right gained in both periods, lower-right gained recently after losing previously, and upper-left lost recently after gaining previously. 03 / Strong setups that move together

X is each stock’s daily-return correlation to your reference, from −1 to +1. Y is its technical score. Upper-right stocks combine strong setups with similar daily moves to the reference. The reference itself is excluded.

Hover or select a point for stock details.

How to read this graph

+1 means perfectly aligned linear daily-return movements; 0 means little linear relationship; −1 means opposite linear movements. The shaded region is correlation ≥0.75 and score ≥8. These are research-screen thresholds, not statistical significance tests. High correlation to one reference does not imply every stock in that area is highly correlated with every other stock; group colors require every member pair to meet 0.75.

Remove market effect fits a separate regression of each stock on SPY and compares the residual returns. This checks co-movement beyond the stock’s estimated SPY exposure. Both performance graphs keep actual returns. Historical correlation can change and does not establish causation.

Groups require every member pair to have correlation ≥0.75; Remove market effect compares residual daily returns after a separate regression of each stock on SPY. Correlations use up to 252 common daily returns, with at least 126 required. The two performance plots show actual stock returns, even when the market-effect toggle changes the correlation groups. Returns use trading days, not calendar days. Historical correlation does not establish causation or predict future diversification.

Research behind these views

These graphs separate past performance, current technical strength, and correlation of matched daily returns. Correlation depends on the sample and return frequency; it is not a permanent property of a stock.

  • MathWorks: comparing stock-return series
  • NIST: interpreting scatter relationships
  • Plerou et al.: market-wide and group return correlations
  • CFA Institute: correlation depends on return frequency
  • CFA Institute: testing signals without look-ahead bias
How scores are calculated

Each stock receives the same independent, rules-based score. Scores run from 1.0 to 10.0; they are technical setup assessments, not return probabilities.

Trend 30%Momentum 20%Relative strength 20%Entry / risk 20%Volume 10%

Trend: position and slope of the 50/200-day averages. Momentum: RSI and MACD histogram. Relative strength: 21/63-session excess return versus SPY. Entry/risk: extension above the 20-day average, distance to a support-based stop reference, and ATR. Volume: 20-session directional volume balance. Component values are 0–100; final score = 1 + 9 × weighted components / 100. Yahoo adjusted daily history is preferred, with a verified Nasdaq fallback when needed. Each row identifies its provider and price basis; stock and SPY comparison always use the same adjustment basis. Corporate actions may affect unadjusted fallback indicators. The latest bar may be intraday. RSI uses exponentially smoothed gains/losses after 14 observations.

8–10: strong setup · 6–7.9: constructive · 4–5.9: mixed · below 4: weak. At least 220 sessions, recent data, usable volume, and aligned SPY history are required. Missing data receives no score. The support/stop reference is a chart measure, not an order recommendation. Scores ignore valuation, earnings events, and portfolio suitability.
