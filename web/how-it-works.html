How I work

From a ticker to a note

What happens between typing a name and getting a research note back: where the evidence comes from, who reasons over it, and the points where the work stops rather than guessing.

The work stops, at any step, if

Two securities could match the name

A required source did not answer

Two sources materially disagree

The evidence will not support the call

Where the evidence comes from

Sources are ranked, and the most authoritative one available answers each claim. A price comes from market data, never from a research note that quotes a price; a reported financial comes from the filing, never from a summary of it.

01 Exchange and security master

Identity, listing, share class, currency. Everything else is keyed to the security this step confirms — not to what was typed.

02 SEC filings and issuer material

10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, earnings releases and investor presentations. The official record wins over anyone's description of it.

03 Market and fundamental data

Prices, volume, history, normalised financials, valuation and consensus estimates. Every indicator in a note is computed here from that history — never read off a picture of a chart.

04 Research houses

A house's published equity rating, price target, credit rating and latest note — J.P. Morgan Markets among them. Read on the portal by an entitled reader and recorded here; nothing automates against it and no credential is held. Each house's rating stays in that house's own words, because an Overweight is not this firm's Buy.

05 News and public sentiment

Current developments, and public discussion where it is legible. Sentiment is context. It never justifies a rating on its own.

How the reasoning is split

The technical and fundamental workstreams run separately, and neither sees the other's conclusion before reaching its own. That is deliberate: shown a rating first, the second opinion tends to become an argument for it rather than a test of it.

The Lead Analyst then reconciles rather than averages, weighting the two by the horizon asked for. A short-horizon question leans on price structure; a long-horizon one leans on the business. When the two disagree — a strong company in a falling chart is the common case — the note says so plainly instead of splitting the difference and calling it a Hold.

What the ratings mean

Seven labels, and two of them are constructive for different readers. Buy speaks to a position that does not exist yet. Add speaks to one that does, usually on a stated condition rather than at any price today — so someone holding the name and someone holding none should not act identically on the same word.

Rating Speaks to What it means
Strong Buy Initiating a position The evidence is aligned across the workstreams and the entry is favourable now. Supports initiating a full position at current levels.
Buy Initiating a position Constructive, with no material dissent that changes the conclusion. Supports initiating a position at current levels.
Add Increasing an existing position Constructive, but qualified. Supports increasing an existing position rather than initiating one here — usually on a stated condition, such as a pullback into the entry zone or the confirmation the note names.
Hold Holding what is held The evidence supports neither adding nor reducing. Maintain the position as it stands and watch the stated triggers.
Reduce Trimming an existing position The evidence has deteriorated but does not require exit. Supports trimming the position, not closing it.
Sell Closing a position The case the position was held on no longer holds. Supports exiting.
Avoid Not owning it Not a position this analysis supports holding at any size at present, whether or not one is already held.

Each states the weight of evidence for a course of action at the stated horizon, on the evidence cited, at the time on the note. None is a forecast. A research house's rating is not one of these — an Overweight is that house's word, on its own scale, and is always shown as theirs. Rating policy 1.0.

What it will not do

  • × Fill a gap with a guess. A figure that could not be retrieved is reported missing, with the reason. It is never quietly replaced by an estimate.
  • × Read a number off a chart image. Indicators are computed from price history. If the history is not there, the indicator is not reported.
  • × Bypass a paywall or a login. Licensed sources are reached through the entitlement the reader already holds, or not at all.
  • × Resolve a material conflict by itself. When two sources disagree on a price or a figure, both are shown with their timestamps and a person decides.
  • × Change its own conclusions from feedback. Feedback is logged for a person to read. It never edits a rating.

Ratings, levels and scenarios are evidence-based assessments, not guarantees, and investing involves risk including possible loss of principal. Internal use only — not for client distribution.
