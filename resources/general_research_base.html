Your question, answered

Wait
Your question

“I have a portfolio that is 90% equities and 20% of that is technology. Is buying TSLA a good decision?”

Direct answer

Not at full size today. Technology already represents roughly 18% of the total portfolio, so adding Tesla would increase both sector concentration and single-stock volatility while the shares remain below their longer-term trend. Wait for better confirmation, or use only a deliberately small starter position if the portfolio can absorb the added risk.

Why

01

Concentration comes first

Your portfolio already carries meaningful technology exposure. Tesla adds a volatile single-stock bet on top of that sleeve rather than improving diversification.

02

The timing is unconfirmed

TSLA has lagged SPY materially and remains below its 50-day and 200-day averages. A bounce has started, but the evidence does not yet support chasing it.

03

Upside is not free

Consensus still points to upside, but the valuation is demanding and earnings growth is mixed. That makes entry discipline more important than the headline target.

What we should do

New buyer
Wait for confirmation

Do not place a full-size order here. Reassess after a sustained close above $361.43 with improving momentum.

If exposure is essential
Use a small starter

Cap the initial position deliberately, then add only if price confirms. Do not let TSLA quietly become a second technology allocation.

Existing holder
Review position size

Keep the holding only if its volatility and drawdown risk fit the total plan. A close below $344.50 calls for a fresh thesis review.

What changes the answer: a sustained close above $361.43, improving MACD, and no breach of the intended technology-allocation ceiling.
