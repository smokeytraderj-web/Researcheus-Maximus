The call

Reduce

Tesla has given back 19.7% over three months while the S&P 500 gained 2.2%, and price now sits below both its 50-day and 200-day averages. We are not adding here. Existing holders should review position size and consider protective hedging; new capital waits for a close above $361.43 with momentum confirming.

The stock is not broken, it is unconfirmed. A 60-day floor held at $297.38 and the one-month bounce is +18.9%, so a base may be forming. But the trend averages have crossed the wrong way and there is no evidence yet that the advance has resumed. The plan below defines exactly what would change our mind and exactly where the idea fails.

Evidence

Four charts, one panel
Read

Price at $354.81 is below the 50-day average of $361.43 and well below the 200-day at $401.41. Until price closes back above $361.43, the chart does not support a durable advance.

MACD has crossed back above its signal line and RSI has recovered to a neutral 53.9. That is early stabilisation, not a buy signal; momentum is the factor most likely to change the rating.

TSLA led the index for most of the year and gave the entire lead back in one move. Its 21.9-point three-month deficit to SPY supports reducing rather than holding through.

Fundamentals

Balanced

Revenue grew 25.5% while earnings fell 3.0%. Sales are expanding but that growth is not reaching the bottom line, which points to margin and cost pressure rather than a demand problem. At a forward P/E of 164.4, the multiple already assumes that gap closes. Leverage is not a concern at a debt/equity of 18.4.

Wall Street's mean target of $390.09 implies 9.9% upside from spot and consensus reads Buy, which sits notably above our own technical read. That divergence is itself the risk to this call.
